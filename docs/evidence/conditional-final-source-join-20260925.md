## Production Integration

The conditional Direct branch retains the actual original ranked owner,
collector/reference bindings, bound B and checked I. Existing constructors build
J/K/P/H/L/R/F once, on the original target account with the caller's original
refinement/forwarding limits. No alternate optimizer or compiler route is added.
Ordinary Direct/UnitLocal and fixed policy6 retain their existing routes.

The lower checker checks N-to-I once, then independently replays the complete
B/C/S/O/I/J/K/P/H/L/R/F history. Prefix endpoints must be the actual borrowed
owners and records, not equal-byte substitutes. Final read/store associations
follow the checked transformation origins rather than assumed stable SSA IDs
or read order. The complete original ordered runtime premises remain required.

The backend owns full retained capacities and checks their prepaid floor on the
original account; borrowed semantic history alone cannot establish that custody.
The existing genuine source/proof/contract replay encloses the new agreement
check. Private F custody is installed only after enclosing source replay and
target-account postchecks. Success of the lower check is unit, not an ordinary
formal proof or source owner, and the production branch still refuses finalization.

Policy7 uses one shared record visitor/encoder for ordinary and conditional
preparation. Ordinary bytes, allocation order and work charges are unchanged.
Conditional preparation additionally binds actual I to J and requires their
complete prepaid storage; it does not manufacture ordinary admission.

Current conditional coverage remains dynamic D1, readonly global slice reads,
one global output store, and an acyclic entry without block arguments. Conditional
entry loops and private-load elimination remain unsupported. Tests exercise
actual value sharing and helper preheader, hoisting, induction and forwarding
rewrites on gfx942/gfx950 graphs. The helper cases are uncalled helpers in the
same module, not evidence of conditional-loop support or GPU execution.
